What materials do you need to tie a Elk Hair Caddis?

6 components · full recipe

To tie a Elk Hair Caddis you need 6 materials: hook, thread, rib, body, hackle, wing. The Elk Hair Caddis is the definitive adult caddis dry fly — buoyant, visible on broken water, and forgiving to tie. The elk hair wing does double duty as flotation and silhouette.

Full material list

ComponentWhat to use
HookStandard dry fly hook, size 12–18
ThreadTan, brown or olive, 8/0
RibFine gold wire (optional)
BodyDubbing — hare's ear, tan or olive
HackleBrown or ginger, palmered over the body
WingElk hair, cleaned and stacked

What each material does

Hook. Fine wire is essential — this pattern floats on the wing and hackle, not on treatment.

Thread. Fine thread matters: elk hair is bulky and thick thread crowds the head.

Rib. Counter-wrapped to protect the palmered hackle.

Body. Dub sparsely; a slim body sits better in the film.

Hackle. Palmering gives the fly its footprint on the surface.

Wing. Stack it properly — uneven tips are the most common fault on this fly.

Which hook suits a Elk Hair Caddis?

A Elk Hair Caddis is tied on a caddis hook. The Woolly Bug database lists 107 hooks suited to this style — see best hooks for caddis patterns for the full list and what the specifications mean.

Which colours should you tie?

Tan and olive cover most hatches. Darker bodies for autumn caddis, lighter for summer.

size 12–18 covers most situations. Smaller sizes for clear water and selective fish, larger for coloured water.

Can you substitute materials in a Elk Hair Caddis?

Yes — most components have workable substitutes, and colour is the most flexible variable. The one thing worth matching properly is the hook style, because it determines the finished fly's proportions.
