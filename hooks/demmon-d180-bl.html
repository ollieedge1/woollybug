Demmon D180 BL

Dry Fly · Sizes #10-18

The Demmon D180 BL is a barbless dry fly hook available in sizes #10-18. It's built around a standard shank, which matches the proportions of most classic dry fly and nymph recipes — if a pattern doesn't specify a shank length, this is what it assumes, paired with standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications. Tyers reach for it most often when working on dry fly, cdc, mayflies. The sections below break down what each specification actually means at the vice, which patterns suit it, and which hooks from other manufacturers you can substitute when it's out of stock.

What do these specifications mean at the vice?

Shank length (Std.). This hook has a standard shank, which matches the proportions of most classic dry fly and nymph recipes — if a pattern doesn't specify a shank length, this is what it assumes.

Wire strength (Std.). It's made from standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications.

Bend and gape (Std.). The D180 BL uses a standard gape, which keeps the fly's proportions traditional.

Eye type (Down). It's finished with a down eye, the traditional choice, which sets a good hooking angle for dry and wet flies fished on a tippet.

Barbless. This hook is barbless from the factory, so it's ready for competition use and catch-and-release waters, and it does noticeably less damage to fish. Keep a steady line tension when playing a fish, as barbless hooks drop out more readily on slack.

What can you tie on a Demmon D180 BL?

The Demmon D180 BL is tagged in the Woolly Bug database for dry fly, cdc, mayflies, caddis. Patterns that sit naturally on this hook include Adams, Parachute Adams, Royal Wulff, CDC Dun, CDC Emerger, CDC & Elk. Because the hook's proportions drive the finished fly's silhouette, it's worth matching the pattern's intended shape to the shank rather than forcing a recipe onto the wrong blank.

What size Demmon D180 BL should you use?

The D180 BL runs from size 10 through 18. The smaller end of the range (down to size 18) covers midge and small mayfly work, where fine tying thread and sparse dressings matter more than bulk. If you're buying this hook for the first time, the mid-range sizes see the most use at the vice, and tracking exactly which sizes you own is where an inventory app earns its keep — it's the detail most tyers forget at the fly shop counter.

What can you substitute for the Demmon D180 BL?

If the Demmon D180 BL is out of stock, the Woolly Bug app lists 5 comparable hooks. When substituting, the specifications that matter most are gape and shank length — these determine whether the finished fly keeps its intended proportions. Wire strength is the next consideration, since it changes how the fly sits in the water: a heavier wire will sink a pattern that was designed to float. Eye type is usually the most forgiving difference, though switching to or from a jig eye changes how the hook rides entirely.
