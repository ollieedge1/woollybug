TMC 3761 Bronze

Wet Fly/Nymphs · Sizes #6-20

The TMC 3761 Bronze is a barbed wet fly/nymphs hook available in sizes #6-20. It's built around a shank one size longer than standard, leaving extra room for a dubbed body or a slightly extended abdomen, paired with notably heavy wire that sinks well and stands up to sustained pressure. Tyers reach for it most often when working on nymphs, beadheads, subsurface. The sections below break down what each specification actually means at the vice, which patterns suit it, and which hooks from other manufacturers you can substitute when it's out of stock.

What these specifications mean at the vice

Shank length (1XL). This hook has a shank one size longer than standard, leaving extra room for a dubbed body or a slightly extended abdomen.

Wire strength (2XH). It's made from notably heavy wire that sinks well and stands up to sustained pressure.

Bend and gape (Sproat). The 3761 Bronze uses a Sproat bend — a classic rounded profile known for strength and reliable holding.

Eye type (Down). It's finished with a down eye, the traditional choice, which sets a good hooking angle for dry and wet flies fished on a tippet.

Barbed. This is a barbed hook. Many competition rules and an increasing number of catch-and-release fisheries require barbless, so check local regulations — the barb can be pinched down with flat-jawed pliers before you dress the hook.

What to tie on it

The TMC 3761 Bronze is tagged in the Woolly Bug database for nymphs, beadheads, subsurface. Patterns that sit naturally on this hook include Hare's Ear Nymph, Pheasant Tail Nymph, Copper John, Beadhead Hare's Ear, Beadhead Pheasant Tail, Diawl Bach. Because the hook's proportions drive the finished fly's silhouette, it's worth matching the pattern's intended shape to the shank rather than forcing a recipe onto the wrong blank.

Choosing a size

The 3761 Bronze runs from size 6 through 20. The smaller end of the range (down to size 20) covers midge and small mayfly work, where fine tying thread and sparse dressings matter more than bulk. Substituting this hook

If the TMC 3761 Bronze is out of stock, the closest match in the database is the Daiichi 1560. When substituting, the specifications that matter most are gape and shank length — these determine whether the finished fly keeps its intended proportions. Wire strength is the next consideration, since it changes how the fly sits in the water: a heavier wire will sink a pattern that was designed to float. Eye type is usually the most forgiving difference, though switching to or from a jig eye changes how the hook rides entirely.
