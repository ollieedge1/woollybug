Best hooks for dry flies

117 hooks from 16 manufacturers

The Woolly Bug database lists 117 hooks suited to dry flies, across 16 manufacturers. The most common specification in this category is a Std. shank with Std. wire, and 58 of the 117 are barbless. Dry fly hooks need to be light. Every gram of wire is weight the hackle and floatant have to hold up, so fine-wire hooks in the 1XF and 2XF classes dominate this category. A standard shank keeps classic proportions, and a down eye gives a clean hooking angle on a fine tippet.

What specifications matter for dry flies?

Shank length. Most hooks in this category use a standard shank, which matches the proportions of most classic dry fly and nymph recipes — if a pattern doesn't specify a shank length, this is what it assumes.

Wire strength. The typical choice here is standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications.

Barbed or barbless. 58 of the 117 hooks listed for dry flies are barbless from the factory. Competition rules and many catch-and-release fisheries require barbless, and a barbed hook can be pinched down with flat-jawed pliers before dressing.
