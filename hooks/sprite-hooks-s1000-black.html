Sprite Hooks S1000 Black

Predator Swimming Shrimp · Sizes #1-8

The Sprite Hooks S1000 Black is a barbed predator swimming shrimp hook available in sizes #1-8. It's built around its shank profile, paired with standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications. Tyers reach for it most often when working on shrimp, saltwater crustacean. The sections below break down what each specification actually means at the vice, which patterns suit it, and which hooks from other manufacturers you can substitute when it's out of stock.

What do these specifications mean at the vice?

Wire strength (Std.). It's made from standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications.

Bend and gape (Continuous). The S1000 Black uses a continuous bend with no defined corner, giving a smooth grub or pupa silhouette.

Eye type (Straight). It's finished with a straight (ring) eye, the most versatile option — it keeps the fly tracking in line with the leader, which suits nymphs and streamers.

Barbed. This is a barbed hook. Many competition rules and an increasing number of catch-and-release fisheries require barbless, so check local regulations — the barb can be pinched down with flat-jawed pliers before you dress the hook.

What can you tie on a Sprite Hooks S1000 Black?

The Sprite Hooks S1000 Black is tagged in the Woolly Bug database for shrimp, saltwater crustacean. Patterns that sit naturally on this hook include Czech Nymph, Pink Shrimp, Crazy Charlie, Merkin Crab. Because the hook's proportions drive the finished fly's silhouette, it's worth matching the pattern's intended shape to the shank rather than forcing a recipe onto the wrong blank.

What size Sprite Hooks S1000 Black should you use?

The S1000 Black runs from size 1 through 8. This is a larger-size range, better suited to attractor patterns, streamers and bigger quarry than to delicate imitative work. If you're buying this hook for the first time, the mid-range sizes see the most use at the vice, and tracking exactly which sizes you own is where an inventory app earns its keep — it's the detail most tyers forget at the fly shop counter.
