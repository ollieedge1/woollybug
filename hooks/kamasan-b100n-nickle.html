Kamasan B100N Nickle

Shrimp/Buzzers · Sizes #10–16

The Kamasan B100N Nickle is a barbed shrimp/buzzers hook available in sizes #10–16. It's built around a shank one size shorter than standard, giving a compact profile that suits short-bodied naturals like midges, buzzers and small emergers, paired with standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications. Tyers reach for it most often when working on shrimp, saltwater crustacean. What do these specifications mean at the vice?

Shank length (1XS). This hook has a shank one size shorter than standard, giving a compact profile that suits short-bodied naturals like midges, buzzers and small emergers.

Wire strength (Std.). It's made from standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications.

Eye type (Down). It's finished with a down eye, the traditional choice, which sets a good hooking angle for dry and wet flies fished on a tippet.

Barbed. This is a barbed hook. Many competition rules and an increasing number of catch-and-release fisheries require barbless, so check local regulations — the barb can be pinched down with flat-jawed pliers before you dress the hook.

What can you tie on a Kamasan B100N Nickle?

The Kamasan B100N Nickle is tagged in the Woolly Bug database for shrimp, saltwater crustacean. Patterns that sit naturally on this hook include Czech Nymph, Pink Shrimp, Crazy Charlie, Merkin Crab. Because the hook's proportions drive the finished fly's silhouette, it's worth matching the pattern's intended shape to the shank rather than forcing a recipe onto the wrong blank.

What can you substitute for the Kamasan B100N Nickle?

If the Kamasan B100N Nickle is out of stock, the Woolly Bug app lists 2 comparable hooks. When substituting, the specifications that matter most are gape and shank length — these determine whether the finished fly keeps its intended proportions. Wire strength is the next consideration, since it changes how the fly sits in the water: a heavier wire will sink a pattern that was designed to float. Eye type is usually the most forgiving difference, though switching to or from a jig eye changes how the hook rides entirely.
