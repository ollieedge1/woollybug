What materials do you need to tie a Woolly Bugger?

7 components · full recipe

To tie a Woolly Bugger you need 7 materials: hook, thread, weight, tail, rib, body, hackle. The Woolly Bugger is the most widely tied fly in the world and the pattern most tyers learn first. It suggests everything and imitates nothing in particular — leech, baitfish, crayfish, large nymph — which is exactly why it keeps catching fish.

Full material list

ComponentWhat to use
HookLong-shank streamer hook, size 6–12
ThreadBlack or colour-matched, 6/0 or 8/0
WeightLead wire or tungsten bead (optional)
TailMarabou, matched to body colour
RibFine copper or gold wire (optional)
BodyChenille — black, olive or brown
HackleSaddle hackle, palmered

What each material does

Hook. A 2XL or 3XL shank carries the marabou tail and chenille body without crowding the eye.

Thread. 6/0 is easier for beginners; 8/0 gives a neater head.

Weight. Weight determines how deep the fly fishes. A bead head also adds a trigger point.

Tail. Roughly one shank length. Marabou is what gives the Bugger its movement at rest.

Rib. Counter-wrapped over the hackle to stop it being cut by a fish's teeth.

Body. Medium chenille for sizes 6–10, fine for smaller.

Hackle. Webby hackle moves more in the water than stiff dry fly hackle.

Which hook suits a Woolly Bugger?

A Woolly Bugger is tied on a streamers hook. The Woolly Bug database lists 74 hooks suited to this style — see best hooks for streamers for the full list and what the specifications mean.

Which colours should you tie?

Black, olive and brown cover most situations. Black for coloured water, olive to suggest a leech or damsel, brown for a crayfish or sculpin.

Frequently asked questions

What materials do you need to tie a Woolly Bugger?

A Woolly Bugger needs 7 components: Hook (Long-shank streamer hook, size 6–12); Thread (Black or colour-matched, 6/0 or 8/0); Weight (Lead wire or tungsten bead (optional)); Tail (Marabou, matched to body colour); Rib (Fine copper or gold wire (optional)); Body (Chenille — black, olive or brown); Hackle (Saddle hackle, palmered).

What hook do you use for a Woolly Bugger?

Long-shank streamer hook, size 6–12. A 2XL or 3XL shank carries the marabou tail and chenille body without crowding the eye.

What size Woolly Bugger should you tie?

size 6–12 covers most situations. Smaller sizes for clear water and selective fish, larger for coloured water.

Can you substitute materials in a Woolly Bugger?

Yes — most components have workable substitutes, and colour is the most flexible variable. The one thing worth matching properly is the hook style, because it determines the finished fly's proportions.
