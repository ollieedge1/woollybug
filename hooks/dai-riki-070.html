Dai-Riki #070

Nymphs/Wet Fly · Sizes #8-18

The Dai-Riki #070 is a barbed nymphs/wet fly hook available in sizes #8-18. It's built around a standard shank, which matches the proportions of most classic dry fly and nymph recipes — if a pattern doesn't specify a shank length, this is what it assumes, paired with standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications. Tyers reach for it most often when working on nymphs, beadheads, subsurface. The sections below break down what each specification actually means at the vice, which patterns suit it, and which hooks from other manufacturers you can substitute when it's out of stock.

What do these specifications mean at the vice?

Shank length (Std.). This hook has a standard shank, which matches the proportions of most classic dry fly and nymph recipes — if a pattern doesn't specify a shank length, this is what it assumes.

Wire strength (Std.). It's made from standard wire — a balance of strength and weight that works across most freshwater applications.

Bend and gape (Sproat). The #070 uses a Sproat bend — a classic rounded profile known for strength and reliable holding.

Eye type (Down). It's finished with a down eye, the traditional choice, which sets a good hooking angle for dry and wet flies fished on a tippet.

Barbed. This is a barbed hook. Many competition rules and an increasing number of catch-and-release fisheries require barbless, so check local regulations — the barb can be pinched down with flat-jawed pliers before you dress the hook.

What can you tie on a Dai-Riki #070?

The Dai-Riki #070 is tagged in the Woolly Bug database for nymphs, beadheads, subsurface. Patterns that sit naturally on this hook include Hare's Ear Nymph, Pheasant Tail Nymph, Copper John, Beadhead Hare's Ear, Beadhead Pheasant Tail, Diawl Bach. Because the hook's proportions drive the finished fly's silhouette, it's worth matching the pattern's intended shape to the shank rather than forcing a recipe onto the wrong blank.

What size Dai-Riki #070 should you use?

The #070 runs from size 8 through 18. The smaller end of the range (down to size 18) covers midge and small mayfly work, where fine tying thread and sparse dressings matter more than bulk. What can you substitute for the Dai-Riki #070?

If the Dai-Riki #070 is out of stock, the Woolly Bug app lists 15 comparable hooks. When substituting, the specifications that matter most are gape and shank length — these determine whether the finished fly keeps its intended proportions. Wire strength is the next consideration, since it changes how the fly sits in the water: a heavier wire will sink a pattern that was designed to float. Eye type is usually the most forgiving difference, though switching to or from a jig eye changes how the hook rides entirely.
